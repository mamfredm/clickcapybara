Search terms, classified by AI

What the script does for you every day.

Most Google Ads accounts lose budget every day to search terms that will never convert: job seekers, competitors, irrelevant queries. Reviewing them by hand takes time and quickly gets out of hand.

Every day the Fetcher pulls your latest search terms into a Google Sheet. The Analyzer sends them to an AI that classifies each term based on your industry, your business and your conversion data.

Every term gets a classification, a confidence score and a one-sentence reason. You review, correct where needed and push the negatives back into your account with one click.

Conversion protection, always on. A search term that has converted is never flagged as a negative.

Three parts, one system

Everything runs inside your own accounts.

01 / Google Ads script

The Fetcher

Runs daily. Pulls every search term from your Search campaigns, removes duplicates and adds up clicks and conversions over 30 days. Writes everything to the sheet.

02 / Apps Script

The Analyzer

Sends the search terms to the AI in batches. Each term comes back with a classification, a confidence score and a plain-language reason. You define your industry once.

03 / Google Sheet

The template

Four tabs: SearchTerms is your working surface, Config is the only thing you adjust per account, Negative_Export holds the validated negatives, and Expansion_Ideas collects terms worth building campaigns around.

Local AI or cloud AI

You choose where your search terms are analysed.

The quick way

Cloud AI

You enter the API key of an AI provider and can start right away. The search terms are sent to that provider for analysis. The cost is usually a few cents per run.

The private way

Local AI

The model runs on your own hardware. The search terms never leave your premises. You need a machine that can run a language model, and a little more setup.

In both cases

Your account stays yours

The script runs in your Google Ads account and your Google Sheet. There is no server of mine in between, and I do not see your data.
